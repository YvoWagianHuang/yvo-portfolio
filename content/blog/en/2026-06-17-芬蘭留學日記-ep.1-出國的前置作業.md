There is really very little information online about studying abroad in Finland. I have finally finished dealing with the tedious administrative tasks recently, so I'm writing a quick article to share with everyone.

This article will be more like an outline. If anyone wants to read about the details of each task, I will write detailed articles later to explain further. (I will do my best to keep updating!)

I will write according to my order of preparation, but these steps can be adjusted. You can complete these tasks based on your own situation, but all of them need to be completed in the end.

### 1. Renting an Apartment

Tampere University does not have student dormitories, but they collaborate with TOAS. TOAS is similar to a student housing platform, and most students rent through them. After you get your exchange student acceptance, the school will email you to remind you to apply for housing on TOAS. One thing to be very careful about:

**TOAS apartments must be grabbed quickly! It's just like snatching concert tickets! So be sure to mark down the exact time.**

When I applied, the website crashed due to technical issues and was down for two hours. In the end, I had to try again the next day, but I still managed to apply successfully. (The English interface gets congested easily; switching to the Finnish interface makes it run smoother.)

After that, you pay the deposit, which can be done in TWD or USD. TOAS will send you a payment link, followed by another link for you to sign the contract online.

> 💡 **Remember to download the digital copy of the lease agreement, as the download link has an expiration date.**

### 2. Insurance

I bought my insurance from AON. It's very quick to complete on their official website, and there are plenty of tutorials online.

### 3. Buying Plane Tickets

I bought my Finnair student tickets through Golden Foundation Travel Service, because buying Finnair tickets on your own can be quite difficult. Processing through a travel agency is much faster, and it's cheaper than buying directly from the official website.

This article was written in 2026, coinciding with the Russia-Ukraine War and changes in the Middle East, so many flight routes are affected. Transferring in the Schengen Area also involves clearing Schengen entry immigration first, so **it is best to transit to Finland via a non-Schengen area**.

### 4. Residence Permit

Taiwanese passport holders enjoy 90-day visa-free entry to Finland. However, if you plan to stay for a semester, you must book an appointment to apply for a residence permit in advance. The fee for a first-time study residence permit is €600.

If possible, book an earlier slot; otherwise, having to carry your little green passport everywhere can still feel a bit nerve-wracking.

### 5. Proof of Financial Means

According to the latest regulations, exchange students must have at least **€800** per month. For example, if you plan to stay for 6 months, your account must have at least €4,800.

You can apply for an English proof of financial balance at your bank and explain that it's for studying abroad. Usually, the bank tellers know exactly what document you need.
