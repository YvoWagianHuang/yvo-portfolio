Recently, it's college application season, and many students from Yili Education have returned to seek college counseling from Yvo. In the vast sea of academia, people always find it difficult to find a major they truly love.

The question they ask most often is: "**Is this major suitable for me? I don't know what I like.**"

To address this, Yvo has compiled five principles and mindsets for reference, hoping to help you find a clearer direction when filling out your preferences:

### 1. Give up the greedy idea of "getting it right on the first try"
It is almost impossible for you to find the "one true answer" for your whole life at the age of 18. You are very likely to feel lost, and it is entirely possible that you will spend your whole life navigating through confusion, which is completely normal. We must admit that we cannot define a whole life through a single choice.
Therefore, **you only need to focus on the next five to ten years of your future**. Human life naturally undergoes stage-by-stage changes, and you need to learn to accept this compromise.

### 2. Think about "Lifestyle" rather than "Profession"
Do not think about your future paths through stereotyped occupational labels like "engineer" or "doctor." Instead, think about your **ideal lifestyle** and use that as the basis for your choice.
Honestly accept all your desires. Regardless of whether they are realistic right now, be honest with yourself and write them down. For example: you want a lot of money, plenty of time for dating, or a highly respected social status. Next, think: "How can I achieve all of this?"

### 3. Find the shortest path to your ideal life
Think about how to get what you want. If "making a lot of money" is the life you desire most, accept that you might need to study STEM fields; if your interest really makes it hard to earn a living, you should consider immigrating early to a country where you can make a living off that interest.
Conversely, if the life you want is "deep connection with people," then you can learn more about which majors and work styles align with that.

### 4. Make good use of the "Process of Elimination"
If you really don't know what you "want," first think about what you "don't want." From a psychological standpoint, people are usually more sensitive to loss and things they dislike, which can help you quickly eliminate unsuitable options.

### 5. Working backward via two-point convergence
Work backward from your "ideal lifestyle" to "your current situation." Organize the advantages you currently possess and the resources you lack, connect these two points and converge inward, and you will find a clear direction for your next five years.

---

> 💡 **Why think about "five years" instead of ten?**
> Because in five years, you can choose to enter the workforce or go to graduate school. By then, you will have more knowledge, connections, and resources that will help you see a further future. Thinking about ten years from now right now makes it easy to fall into meaningless overthinking and mental exhaustion.
