Recently, while developing an **AI teaching assistant** on the WISE platform, I ran headfirst into an invisible obstacle.

My goal was to have the teaching assistant guide students to think independently rather than directly giving answers. However, during testing, I clearly saw that "**invisible language tax**": with the same logic, the model responded fluently in English, but seemed like a different entity when switched to Chinese. This is because training data for mainstream LLMs remains predominantly English. I couldn't help but feel anxious: if future education is driven by AI, are children who aren't proficient in English destined to be left behind?

Carrying this question, after a lecture at NTNU, I approached **Hiroki Terashima**, founder of the WISE platform and professor at UC Berkeley.

### [Diversity in Design: The Invisibility Cloak of Bias]

I asked Hiroki point-blank: Will this data-driven linguistic divide make educational resources even more unequal?

His answer was a wake-up call. He didn't talk about algorithms; he talked about "people." He said:

> "Diversity in Design is not an option; it is a necessity."

He explained that if a development team has a homogenous background, AI bias acts like it's wearing an "**invisibility cloak**," disappearing from the developers' sight. Only by introducing "**gatekeepers**" from different cultures and native languages can we pinpoint errors before the model harms specific groups. In that high-level dialogue, I felt the "flow" of my brain running at full capacity—my purest passion for **EdTech**.

### [From Hardware to Systems: Being a Trailblazer of the Era]

To my delight, as the lecture ended, Hiroki actually sought me out in the crowd to exchange contact information and expressed interest in hearing more of my insights.

From him, I saw that the value of an **EdTech** expert is not just tuning models, but enabling technology to possess "**empathy**." Contemporary educational technology has crossed from the hardware level of "smartboards" to the architectural dimension of **AI systems**.

> I will continue to be the person who dares to challenge the algorithm, ensuring that technological progress serves to dismantle social stratification rather than deepen divides.

**Beyond the Algorithm: A Dialogue on AI Equity with Hiroki Terashima**

### [The "Language Tax" Anxiety]

While developing an **AI teaching assistant** on the WISE platform, I encountered an invisible barrier.

My goal was to create an AI that guides students toward inquiry rather than just providing answers. However, I observed a recurring "**Linguistic Tax**": models that are articulate and logical in English often lose their nuance in Chinese. This data bias, rooted in English-centric training sets, keeps me up at night: If the future of education is AI-driven, are non-native English speakers destined to be left behind?

I took this concern to Professor **Hiroki Terashima** (UC Berkeley), the founder of WISE, during his session at NTNU.

### [Diversity as a "Safety Feature"]

I asked Hiroki point-blank: Will this data-driven linguistic gap exacerbate educational inequality?

His response shifted my perspective entirely. He didn't talk about code; he talked about humanity.

> "Diversity in Design is not a choice; it is a necessity," stated.

He explained that in a homogenous team, bias acts like an "**invisibility cloak**," undetected by those within the bubble. We need "**gatekeepers**" from diverse linguistic and cultural backgrounds to detect failures before they harm learners. Engaging in such a high-level exchange put me in a state of "flow"—a moment where my passion for **EdTech** felt more vivid than ever.

### [The Future: Empathy-Driven Systems]

To my surprise, as the session ended, Hiroki sought me out in the crowd to exchange contact information, eager to hear more about my insights.

Hiroki embodies the true value of an **EdTech** expert: it’s not just about building "smarter" models, but building "**empathetic**" systems. Modern EdTech has evolved from hardware like smartboards into complex **AI ecosystems**.

> I am committed to being the one who challenges the algorithm—not just as a developer, but as a pioneer ensuring that technology serves as a bridge, not a wall, for every learner.
