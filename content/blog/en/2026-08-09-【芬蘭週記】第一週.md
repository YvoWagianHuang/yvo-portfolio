It has been a week since I arrived in Finland, and I am now settled in. Living alone in Northern Europe feels like a dreamy yet lonely journey. I go out for a walk every day, sometimes strolling in the forest, or simply buying groceries at the supermarket. But the moment I enjoy the most is going to the lake behind my apartment, watching wave after wave build up. The endless surface of the lake brings peace and refreshment to my mind.

![By the lake near my apartment](/images/IMG_5630.jpeg)
> 📷 *By the lake near my apartment*

Finland is a quiet country. From the airport to the buses, there are no unnecessary announcements or public notices at all. Compared to Taiwan, it is much more peaceful without an overload of information bombardment. However, one has to keep a constant eye on the electronic displays, as the buses here don't even announce station names.

In such a serene environment, I have a lot of time every day to organize my thoughts, reflect on the past, and ponder the future, realizing that many small happinesses and wonders in life are quietly blooming around me.

The day before yesterday, while walking in the park, a group of teachers and students from a nearby high school came by for an outdoor lesson. The group was very quiet, slowly moving to stand before each statue to listen to the lecture. Although not every student was paying full attention, if this were in Taiwan, it would surely be noisy, with kids goofing around and getting side-eyes from passersby, while the teacher would have to wear a portable loudspeaker to raise their voice over the clamor. However, the teacher I saw that day was able to complete the lesson using just a normal speaking volume. This left a deep impression on me, making me wonder what kind of magic made it possible.

![Street view and statues in Finland](/images/IMG_5597.jpeg)

I think this is why I came here.

These are a corner of my life. I really look forward to seeing what other surprises Finland will bring me, and what I, in turn, can bring to the people here.
