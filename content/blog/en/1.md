> "An era is like a sieve, filtering people into displacement and hardship." This is a famous line left by Wang Dingjun in *Broken Glass*, and a classic passage in Taiwan's Chinese literature classes.

But I often wonder, isn't modern education just another kind of sieve? Through standardized exams and placement, we precisely categorize students into different tiers. This logic of **"factory production"**—pursuing yield rates, weeding out defective products, and obsessing over college acceptance rates—is precisely the deep concern seen by critical education scholars.

> During my time pursuing the Teacher Education Program at NTNU, I was deeply fascinated by these reflections. In my midterm essay, I proposed a core argument: In an era of declining birthrates, **"collaboration"** is the vital survival skill that education must proactively deploy.

### 1. From "Stock Competition" to "Incremental Value"

The educational logic of the agricultural era was **"resource grabbing"**; because resources were limited, competition had to be venerated. However, modern educational resources in Taiwan are approaching surplus. The real crisis is no longer "failing to get into school," but rather "losing one's soul after getting in." We still retain the remnants of industrial-era sorting while ignoring how, in an age of abundance, collaboration can be used to create a quantum leap in quality where "1+1>2".

### 2. Schools Should Be "Social Scaffolding," Not a "Lonely Arena"

Declining birthrates have turned many children into "princes and princesses on isolated islands." At home, they enjoy abundant resources but lack the experience of dividing labor, sharing, or reconciling after conflicts with siblings. School, as a rare space where children gather, should not merely be another arena for mindlessly grinding practice exams, but rather a functional structure equipped with **"Social Scaffolding."** What we should teach is how children can look others in the eye and harmoniously find consensus amidst disagreement.

### 3. Project Collaboration: The "Hard Skill for the Workplace" Over the Next 30 Years

Looking to the future, the era of the lone-wolf hero is over. Whether dealing with international projects or AI collaboration, this is not merely "socializing," but a **"strategic literacy"** involving project management, emotional resilience, and team dynamics. In the curriculum design of Yili Education, **"collaboration"** is never a random assignment, but rather incorporates **Differentiated Instruction**:

We do not dump tasks onto top performers under the guise of "the capable should do more work." Instead, through thoughtful design, students of all levels can find their own **"Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD)"** within the team, allowing every student to become the hero who leads the team at a specific moment.

> Just like the spirit of the US Navy SEALs: "Ready to lead, ready to follow."

Does your child possess this spirit? Facing an uncertain 2026 or even 2050, competition is certainly important, but **"collaboration"** is unequivocally the most critical survival challenge for the next 30 years.
