Today, while deep cleaning, I stumbled upon my college application documents from five years ago. In the study plan section, I had written down my goal to go to Japan for an exchange program. Coincidentally, the NTNU (National Taiwan Normal University) exchange student program results were released recently, and everything seems to suggest that I should write down some of my preparation experience.

I delayed my graduation and stayed for a fifth year in university—first, to apply for the exchange student program, and second, to complete my teaching internship and build a portfolio for master's applications. Because I only made up my mind to go abroad during the second semester of my senior year, I had only six months to prepare, which was extremely rushed.

If I could talk to my freshman self, I would strongly urge him to do the following. If you are in the early stages of college and eager to go on an exchange program abroad, I hope you will do these as well:

### 1. GPA GPA GPA!!!!

In college, most selection processes take **GPA** into account, including double majors, overseas internships, and studying abroad as an exchange student—GPA carries significant weight in all of them.

During my sophomore year, I got distracted academically due to various factors, so my GPA gradually dropped below 4.0. I believe that applying for an exchange program in your sophomore year is a great timing because you only need to focus on maintaining your grades for one year, and you haven't hit the more advanced upper-division courses yet. In addition, going on exchange earlier allows you to enjoy the long-tail effect sooner, building international perspective and relevant experience earlier.

> 💡 **Grades are truly important!!**  
> Grades represent your academic ability and self-discipline, serving as a relatively objective indicator to measure a student's performance.

### 2. English Proficiency and Language Test Certificates

For many people, their English proficiency peaks in their senior year of high school. After entering college, many people's English slowly but steadily declines, becoming quite rusty by their sophomore year.

This is precisely why I suggested going on exchange early: you want to practice your English to a level capable of going abroad while you still remember high school English. This way, not only is preparation much easier, but you can also get your language test certificate in advance. A TOEFL score report is valid for two years, which covers almost most of your undergraduate career.

No matter how good you think your English is, you need a language test certificate to prove yourself. There are two mainstream English proficiency tests: TOEFL and IELTS. Except for the UK, which mostly requires IELTS, taking either is usually fine. Personally, I think TOEFL is easier to prepare for because the IELTS speaking test is conducted by a human examiner, introducing more variables, whereas TOEFL tests are entirely computer-based.

How important is an English proficiency test, really? Taking myself as an example, because I took the TOEFL, I obtained the following campus opportunities:
- Host English study groups
- Serve as a teaching assistant for EMI (English as a Medium of Instruction) bilingual courses
- Language scholarships
- Exchange student qualification

So, prepare for your English proficiency test as early as possible!

### 3. EMI Courses

Universities offer some courses taught entirely in English (EMI courses). In NTNU's exchange selection, the number of EMI courses accounts for 5% of the total score, requiring 3 courses to get full marks. In recent years, due to the bilingual nation policy, there are also US exchange programs that only consider the number of EMI courses taken, without requiring a TOEFL score. Here is the press release: https://pr.ntnu.edu.tw/ntnunews/index.php?mode=data&id=23988

I strongly recommend taking some EMI courses if you have the chance in college to accumulate the following experiences:
- Holding a cup of coffee while reading a mountain of English papers, repeatedly asking AI for translations
- Writing essay after essay in broken English and making progress along the way
- Trembling while expressing your ideas in English during class discussions
- Writing frantically during midterm and final exams, ignoring grammar rules

You will become braver and braver, gradually mastering academic English and overcoming the fear of foreign languages.

> 💡 **English is the language of wisdom.**  
> Possessing English ability allows you to delve into knowledge within your professional field without relying on translators.

### 4. Accumulate Experiences

Unfortunately, no matter how many glorious achievements you had in the past, you must start fresh in college because most selection processes only consider experiences gained during college and beyond. That's not entirely a bad thing, right? You get a chance to start from scratch.

If you consider yourself a boring person with no special skills other than studying for exams, I will give you a piece of advice: **Be a YES Man / Woman**.

The protagonist in the comedy film *Yes Man* starring Jim Carrey lived a repetitive, increasingly numb life every day. But after attending a self-help seminar where he was challenged to say "YES" to every opportunity, his life became rich and wild.

Of course, the movie also hints at the drawbacks of this lifestyle, so we need to know when to draw the line. What I want to emphasize is that occasional madness might help you find an opportunity to break through the status quo. So, dare to try! Whether it's campus clubs or off-campus competitions, building up more experience is always good.

Most importantly, turn all your experiences into assets and document them in ways that can be presented on paper. In my experience, I have done the following:
- Selected a few group photos with principals and officials taken during internships or events
- Proactively requested event certificates or letters of appreciation from organizers
- Wrote articles and maintained a LinkedIn profile and personal website to document my academic performance
- Developed final term papers into feature reports or short research papers

I started cultivating these small habits during my junior year. Storing these achievements on a cloud drive was immensely helpful when applying for various jobs and programs, as I could always provide concrete proof of my achievements in my resume.

When reading exchange student stories, we always notice their glamorous photos abroad. But looking back on everything, I would say that the preparation and hard work put into it were what changed me the most.

I didn't decide to pursue further studies in Finland at the very beginning. Rather, on the journey of preparing for the exchange, because it was truly so exhausting, I asked myself time and time again: What is all this for? And that is how I found my direction in life.

> 💡 **Meaning is found through action; you must take action to find the meaning of life.**

So, take action! Future college students, I wish you all the best.
