### How do we design educational tools to facilitate authentic, cross-cultural discourse?

In this week’s ERG session, we had the privilege of hosting Mr. Otto, a software engineer from Finland.

To overcome potential language barriers and lower the **"affective filter"**, I developed a custom HTML interactive tool inspired by Japanese **"Karuta"** and memory-matching game mechanics. This **"gamified scaffolding"** successfully guided our dialogue, allowing us to seamlessly explore complex socio-cultural phenomena—from Finnish sauna culture and the adoption of digital tools, all the way to comparing Taiwan's workplace dynamics and habits of social indirectness.

A major highlight of the event was Udella, who has a background in geography, delivering a presentation entirely in English that brilliantly introduced Otto to how Taiwan’s topography and folk customs shape its infrastructure—a perfect demonstration of interdisciplinary **"Phenomenon-based Learning"**!

The session ultimately concluded at an interesting intersection of engineering and cultural anthropology: we gifted Otto a pack of **"Kuai Kuai"**, Taiwan’s unique tech-talisman, to bless his hardware equipment to run smoothly!

> This session once again confirmed that the true pedagogical affordance of Educational Technology (EdTech) lies in building bridges for human experiences to connect.

### 🇫🇮 Cross-Cultural Scaffolding: Gamifying the Finnish-Taiwanese Dialogue

### How do we design educational tools to facilitate authentic, cross-cultural discourse?

In this week’s ERG session, we had the privilege of hosting Mr. Otto, a software engineer from Finland.

To overcome potential language barriers and lower the **affective filter**, I developed a custom HTML-based interactive tool inspired by Japanese **Karuta (花牌)** and memory-matching mechanics. This **"Gamified Scaffolding"** successfully guided our dialogue, allowing us to seamlessly navigate complex socio-cultural phenomenons—comparing Finnish sauna norms and digital tool adoption with Taiwan's workplace dynamics and social indirectness.

A major highlight was our Geography major, Udella, delivering a full-English presentation on how Taiwan’s topology and folk customs shape its infrastructure—a brilliant demonstration of interdisciplinary **Phenomenon-based Learning**. 

We concluded the session at the intersection of engineering and cultural anthropology: gifting Otto a pack of **"Kuai Kuai" (乖乖)**, Taiwan’s unique tech-talisman for keeping hardware running smoothly! 

> This session reaffirmed that EdTech’s true pedagogical affordance lies in bridging human experiences.
