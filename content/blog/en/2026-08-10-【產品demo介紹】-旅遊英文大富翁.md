### 🎮 Design Overview

This is an **educational English digital game** designed for 5th to 8th-grade students. Its core purpose is to allow students to naturally learn travel English through gameplay.

![Game Interface](/images/%E6%88%AA%E5%9C%96%202026-08-10%20%E4%B8%8B%E5%8D%883.41.29.png "Game Interface")

The gameplay is generally similar to traditional Monopoly, but for educational purposes, I made the following modifications:

1. **Step-by-Step Vocabulary Challenges**: Almost every tile landed on triggers an English vocabulary question. Answering correctly offers various rewards, such as purchasing land, reducing toll fees when landing on others' property, or receiving random cash bonuses and items.
2. **High-Frequency Stimulation**: Repeatedly stimulate students' memory through fast-paced gameplay and questions.
3. **Painless Speaking**: The game format significantly reduces the pressure on students to speak English. When encountering questions they don't know, they are much more willing to make educated guesses.
4. **"Hero's Blessing" Mechanism**: Periodically, the student with the lowest funds on the board gains the "Hero's Blessing" status! In this state, one incorrect option is automatically eliminated for every question. This mechanism is designed to support lower-proficiency students, ensuring that no one gives up halfway.
5. **Team Mode**: Students can form 2-player teams to play, sharing assets and land, and discussing questions together.

### 📈 Practical Results

I conducted a month-long course for a group of students using this game. We spent 1.5 hours playing Monopoly each week, alongside a 1-hour one-on-one tutoring session per student weekly, where I used printed handouts to supplement and explain the vocabulary featured in the game.

Around **100 new vocabulary words** appeared in the game, ranging from very basic words like `open` to advanced terms like `economy class`.

When the course first started, students found the paper handouts boring. However, as the course progressed, they realized that the knowledge learned in the one-on-one sessions could be directly applied in the game to gain an advantage, becoming extremely engaged! (By the way, they loved every moment when a classmate landed on their property 😂)

> 🏆 **Final Outcome**
> By the end of the course, every student remembered the meaning of at least 50 vocabulary words and could actually apply them in English writing and reading.

### 🔍 My Findings

1. **Teamwork Beats Going Solo**: The students' favorite mode was "2v2 Mode" because they could share notes and solve questions together.
2. **Bridging the Proficiency Gap**: The game design encourages answering questions to earn resources, which naturally favors students with stronger English skills. However, by incorporating random elements like Chance and Fate cards, lower-proficiency students also had a chance to win. This successfully bridged the skill gap and motivated everyone to learn together.

> 💡 **An Unexpected Classroom Discovery:**
> In one session, Student A (who was leading) triggered a natural disaster and lost half their wealth, allowing Student B (who originally had a lower answer accuracy) to make an instant comeback. However, Student A was still able to catch up financially by actively answering questions; meanwhile, Student B, having taken the lead, became even more proactive and attentive in taking paper notes to maintain their advantage.
> **This proves that dynamic game mechanics can simultaneously boost motivation for students of all proficiency levels!**

### 🚀 Future Outlook

1. **Online Multiplayer**: Currently, only local mode is available. In the future, I hope to add online matchmaking capabilities.
2. **Learning Data Analytics**: The game backend already tracks student responses, but currently it only records "answer accuracy" and "most frequent errors." In the future, I hope to implement a more detailed analytics dashboard to help educators accurately identify students' learning blind spots.

### Conclusion: Rediscovering the Warmth of Interaction

When I was a child, I loved playing Monopoly. Gathering around with classmates at school, holding play money and property cards, nervously rolling the dice... these are deeply cherished childhood memories for me.

Children today are gradually losing these opportunities for "face-to-face interactive" gaming, turning instead to online games (which, to some extent, stifles their social opportunities). Travel English Monopoly not only achieved my original teaching goals but also rebuilt a positive environment for interaction—kids would whisper hints in soft tones when others struggled to answer, which was truly heartwarming to watch!

In the past, teaching through board games required spending a lot of time setting up and packing away physical materials. But looking ahead, I know that **Educational Technology (EdTech) will continue to reshape current forms of education**.

Through the power of technology, we can efficiently design more flexible and personalized learning materials, **making everyone fall in love with learning**.
