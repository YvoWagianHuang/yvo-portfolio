### [Foreword]

I have always been deeply grateful for tutoring. It allowed me to be self-sufficient while completing my university studies, and more importantly, it gave me extensive hands-on experience in communicating and dealing with different people. As my career focus gradually shifts from frontline teaching to "curriculum design" and "digital education," I want to organize a few insights to share with juniors who are about to enter this market.

### [1. Focus on One or Two Subjects First]

When starting out, people often teach every subject just to take on more cases. This is understandable in the early stages, but if you want to do this long-term, you must learn to **"focus."** A teacher specializing in English teaching usually holds higher brand value than a "jack-of-all-trades" who teaches everything. When you cultivate a few subjects deeply over a long period, you will develop unique teaching insights—that is where your professionalism lies.

### [2. This is a Job About "People"]

> Please remind yourself at all times: you are usually dealing with minors.

Every word and action of yours, including your biases or habits, will subtly influence your students. We cannot necessarily demand that everyone be gentle, but at the very least, the original intention of teaching must not carry malice.

### [3. Professionalism Lies Not in Job Titles, But in Being "Irreplaceable"]

Having an education background is great, of course, but if you are a newcomer from another field, I suggest writing down "teaching reflections" before and after every class. Compare: where was the gap between your anticipated process and what actually happened? Was the explanation too messy? Were the questions asked poorly? Or was the student's progress ahead of schedule? These gaps are your room for improvement. Making mistakes is not scary; the key is to consciously make corrections. This **"ability for self-optimization"** is true professionalism.

### [4. Can I Be a Tutor if My Academic Credentials Aren't Impressive?]

To be frank, the Taiwanese market indeed values academic credentials. If your credentials aren't impressive, you must compensate for it elsewhere. You can become a teaching assistant at a cram school to accumulate classroom management experience, take theoretical courses in university, or even participate in rural service. Once you have these "real-world stories," your bargaining power will naturally increase.

> Remember rule #3: Professionalism is irreplaceability.

### [5. Do Exam Orientation and Educational Passion Really Conflict?]

Many teachers complain that in order to deal with exams, they cannot teach what they want to teach. But I believe this is a **"false dilemma."** Helping students get high scores and integrating educational philosophy can go hand in hand. On the back cover of Yili Chinese textbooks, four big characters are written: Fortitude, Curiosity, Gentleness, Rationality. When teaching writing skills, we connect stories of basketball players or life experiences. We not only teach students how to take exams, but also infuse reflections on life into the text.

### [6. About Lesson Prep: How to Narrow the Distance Between Students and Their Goals?]

Regarding pedagogy, there are many theories in books; here I share my three thinking principles:

(1) **Concrete Needs**: Where exactly is the student stuck? Is it the application of an equation, or a certain grammar rule?

(2) **Identify the Gap**: What is missing between their current state and the target? Is it insufficient knowledge? Unfamiliar skills? Or simply a lack of a practice environment?

(3) **Design Activities**: What teaching methods or texts can easily fill these gaps without making the student feel overly stressed?

> I recommend trying to write down these thoughts with pen and paper before accumulating 100 hours of experience.

Even though I am now a mature designer, when designing large-scale teaching materials, I still follow this logic to ensure every teaching step is precise.

### [Conclusion]

Teaching is a shared adventure with students. If you are also interested in "instructional design" and even hope to save tedious lesson prep time to fully dedicate yourself to the teaching scene, feel free to contact "Yili Education." We can tailor teaching materials and lesson plans for you. Let's walk the path of education more intelligently and professionally together.

I will continue to update tutoring mindsets in future articles, and I sincerely invite everyone to collaborate with us. No matter how good the teaching materials are, they cannot compare to the actual impact of an excellent teacher on students—only you on the frontline can give soul to the teaching materials.
